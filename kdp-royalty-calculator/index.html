How KDP royalties work

Ebooks earn 70% of the list price when it is between $2.99 and $12.99 on Amazon.com (the upper limit was $9.99 until July 7, 2026). At 70%, Amazon subtracts a delivery cost of $0.15 per megabyte of the file. Outside that band, or if you choose it, the rate is 35% with no delivery cost.

Paperbacks earn 60% of the list price from $9.99 and 50% below it, and the printing cost is subtracted from that share. So a cheap, thick paperback can earn very little: check that the royalty is still worth it before you lower the price.

FAQ

Why does my $8.99 paperback earn less than my $9.99 one?

Below $9.99 the paperback rate drops from 60% to 50%, so a $1 cheaper price can cost you more than $1 in royalty.

Is 70% always better for an ebook?

Almost always within $2.99–$12.99. Only very large files (lots of images) can lose so much to delivery costs that 35% pays more; the calculator shows both.
